# Aurelia Starlight — 3D avatar

Aurelia is a styled derivative of pixiv's VRM 1.0 sample model, with a VStudio celestial palette, an embroidered princess dress and reconstructed torso, fitted shorts and socks, authored Mary Jane shoes, a braided half-up hairstyle, a fitted volumetric crown, crystal earrings, brooch and beaded chains. The underlying anatomy, skinned geometry, painted textures, facial morph targets and spring-bone rig were authored by **pixiv Inc.**, not generated by VStudio.

## Implementation

The renderer uses Three.js and `@pixiv/three-vrm`: skinned meshes, a humanoid bone hierarchy, MToon materials, facial blend shapes, eye bones and spring-bone hair. `src/components/three/aureliaModel.ts` supplies the new materials/accessories and maps the shared tracking rig onto the humanoid. The viewer supports orbit, zoom, frontal reset, turntable and a geometry wireframe.

The torso has measured waist proportions, shape-preserving cross-sections and smooth paired chest contours without a sharp central ridge. Source waist and neck overlap is trimmed at the replacement boundaries; the new torso joins the retained pelvis, arms and neck with their source skin weights. Neck rotation is confined to the medial neck rather than pulling the shoulders. Fitted shorts remain beneath the skirt, and the dress visibility button exposes the body and underlayer for inspection. Mary Jane shoes have rounded toes, straps, buckles, layered soles and low heels; fitted ivory socks retain the source ankle weights.

The princess dress combines ivory brocade, periwinkle panels, floral embroidery, raised metallic stitching, an embroidered petal overskirt, gathered hem layers and openwork lace. Fabric maps are authored procedurally; the lace uses an alpha-cutout material with open spaces between its threads. Short gathered sleeves follow the upper arms, while straps and the choker share the torso's skin weights. The lower bodice includes ease over the fitted shorts. Gold piping is batched separately for the torso, hip sash and simulated skirt to preserve their distinct deformation while limiting draw calls.

The 816-particle skirt uses a fixed 120 Hz Verlet solver with stretch, shear and bending constraints, gravity, damping, shape memory and thigh capsules. The skirt, petal layer, lace and bindings all sample the same simulated material coordinates. Pausing preserves settled folds; manual pose changes rebase the drape without adding artificial velocity. This is lightweight real-time garment dynamics, not an offline fabric or self-collision solver.

The half-up hairstyle adds dimensional crown braids, tapered face-framing locks, a layered rear cascade and a satin bow with ribbon tails. Four added skinned meshes share the source skeleton; moving locks and ribbons reuse its existing spring chains. The original hair and painted strand textures remain present.

Earring studs sit on the measured lobes; linked dimensional pendants hang under gravity with damped sway. The tiara is fitted by raycasting the actual hair shell, including its rear band. Additional bust spheres and shoulder capsules participate in the hair spring-bone simulation and follow the upper chest. Hair and iris pigments preserve source texture detail with runtime material shaders. Body pigment is unified across the original limbs and new torso to remove painted shirt-edge shadows.

Transparent PNG and WebM/GIF recording capture the actual WebGL view. Downloaded GLB contains geometry, skeleton, skin weights, embedded textures and facial morph targets. GLB uses portable PBR materials, so its shading differs from MToon; runtime pigment shaders are not baked by the generic exporter. It is not a VRM export: VRM expression bindings, spring-bone extensions, the garment simulation and this application's procedural animation controller are not encoded by the generic GLB exporter.
