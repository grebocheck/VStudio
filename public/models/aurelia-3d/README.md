# Aurelia Starlight — 3D avatar

Aurelia is a styled derivative of pixiv's VRM 1.0 sample model, with a VStudio celestial palette, original draped dress and reconstructed torso, fitted shorts and socks, authored Mary Jane shoes, a fitted volumetric crown, crystal earrings, brooch and beaded chains. The underlying anatomy, skinned geometry, painted textures, facial morph targets and spring-bone rig were authored by **pixiv Inc.**, not generated by VStudio.

## Implementation

The renderer uses Three.js and `@pixiv/three-vrm`: skinned meshes, a humanoid bone hierarchy, MToon materials, facial blend shapes, eye bones and spring-bone hair. `src/components/three/aureliaModel.ts` supplies the new materials/accessories and maps the shared tracking rig onto the humanoid. The viewer supports orbit, zoom, frontal reset, turntable and a geometry wireframe.

The torso uses a modest pear-shaped chest contour with lower fullness and a gradual upper transition. The dress adds material ease, a bridge across the centre, gentle drape and shallow folds. Shoulder straps and the choker sample the same underlying body surface and share its skin weights. A continuous skinned torso bridges geometry deliberately absent underneath the source shirt. The original pelvis and full legs remain present; only duplicated neck surfaces are trimmed at the measured replacement boundary. Fitted shorts stay beneath the retained skirt. The dress visibility button allows inspection of the complete body and underlayer. Mary Jane shoes have rounded toes, straps, buckles, layered soles and low heels; fitted ivory socks retain the source ankle weights. The bodice's lower waist weights match the skirt's pinned ring. The 816-particle skirt uses a fixed 120 Hz Verlet solver, stretch/shear/bending constraints, gravity, damping, shape memory and thigh capsules. Hem bindings and stitched details sample the same simulated surface. Pausing preserves the settled drape; manual pose changes rebase it without adding artificial velocity. This is lightweight real-time garment dynamics, not an offline fabric or self-collision solver.

Earring studs sit on the measured lobes; linked dimensional pendants hang under gravity with damped sway. The tiara is fitted by raycasting the actual hair shell, including its rear band. Additional bust spheres and shoulder capsules participate in the hair spring-bone simulation and follow the upper chest. Hair and iris pigments preserve source texture detail with runtime material shaders. Body pigment is unified across the original limbs and new torso to remove painted shirt-edge shadows.

Transparent PNG and WebM/GIF recording capture the actual WebGL view. Downloaded GLB contains geometry, skeleton, skin weights, embedded textures and facial morph targets. GLB uses portable PBR materials, so its shading differs from MToon; runtime pigment shaders are not baked by the generic exporter. It is not a VRM export: VRM expression bindings, spring-bone extensions, the garment simulation and this application's procedural animation controller are not encoded by the generic GLB exporter.
